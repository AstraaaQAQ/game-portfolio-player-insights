# Demo Text: The Mine and The Moon

Extracted from the desktop demo text document for portfolio review.

## Main Route

### Start

This is a dystopian city divided into two worlds: the Tower and the Plains.

The Tower is home to the elite, powered by light, order, and abundant resources. Beneath it lie the Plains, where miners, black-market traders, and people abandoned by the system struggle to survive.

The city runs on a resource known as miner crystal.

Sedes Westly was born above, into one of the families that profits from crystal light. Today, he enters the mine as a journalist, collecting samples while investigating why disasters in the Plains mines have become so frequent.

He carries a latest-model camera, a family watch engraved with the Westly crest, and a dangerous curiosity for the world below. The camera is expensive enough to survive most fieldwork. The crest works almost like a pass in the Tower: it opens doors, softens inspections, and makes officials listen.

That curiosity matters too. Sedes wants to know why the official reports always call the deaths accidental, and why everyone seems so eager not to ask.

None of them will mean the same thing by the time he reaches the shaft.

### Node 0

The camera focus tightens on the freshly opened ore.

Sedes expects crystal texture. Instead, deep in the cold, reflective stone, he finds a human finger: pale, grey, and pressed into the vein as if someone shoved it there in a hurry.

A strip of flesh still clings near the nail.

For a moment, Sedes forgets to breathe.

Then the detector begins to scream.

### Node 1

Acid water begins to seep through the lower shaft.

Not natural groundwater. Not rain. A prepared mixture, sharp enough to smoke where it touches the steel floor.

The detectors shriek without pause. Red warning lights tear across the crystal walls. Someone shouts that the pumps are dead. Someone else slips in the rising water and drags two others down with him.

In the panic, an injured miner is shoved to the ground. His glove has split open. The skin beneath is already burning.

Sedes feels three weights pull at him at once.

The family watch inside his glove.

The camera against his chest.

The miner reaching from the acid water.

He has seconds to decide what matters.

### Node 1A

The watch slips in his glove. Sedes catches it before it falls into the acid water.

The cover springs open.

Inside is a yellowed family photograph: the Westlys, young and bright, sitting with him at a picnic inside the Tower. For a second, his parents look almost weightless.

Then the memory changes. His mother beneath the lights of another charity dinner, smiling with perfect lips and empty eyes. His father in formal dress, grey at the temples, speaking polished nonsense for men old enough to own every room they entered.

Sedes closes his fist around the cold gold.

If he gets out with the watch and the camera, his family will not have to keep smiling at the edge of the table forever. The mine disaster. The evidence. The Westly name.

Maybe all of it can still become leverage.

### Node 1B

The detector starts screaming again.

Acid water crawls over the floor, climbing the slope like something alive. Someone swears. Someone yells at him to stop filming and move.

Sedes does not move.

His lens stays fixed on the severed finger inside the ore.

If people can be refined into light, what protection is the Westly name supposed to give him? Today it is miners. Tomorrow it could be an inconvenient minor house. One wrong alliance, one wrong vote, and his family could vanish into the same nameless crystal.

The camera feels different in his hands.

Not equipment.

A fuse.

### Node 1C

The injured miner is still on the floor.

Sedes drops beside him before he can think better of it. Acid water soaks through the knees of his expensive protective suit. The fabric stiffens, then begins to smoke.

Someone shouts that he is insane.

Sedes tears open the outer layer of the suit, then rips a strip from the fine shirt beneath it. The cloth was made for Tower dinners, not blood, not acid, not a man screaming through his teeth.

He presses it hard against the miner's wound.

For the first time since entering the shaft, no one is looking at him like a Westly.

They are looking to see whether he will let go.

### Node 2

Sirens blur into one long metallic scream.

The acid water keeps rising, hissing against the lower beams. Somewhere above, a pipe knocks once, twice, then bursts. The whole shaft answers with a deep, unstable groan.

The air is getting thin.

Sedes tastes copper every time he breathes. His visor fogs at the edges. Someone says the pressure valves are failing. Someone else says if the gas reaches the wrong spark, the mine will not flood.

It will ignite.

He still has the watch.

He still has the evidence.

He still has people screaming around him.

He cannot keep everything.

### Node 2A

Sedes pockets what still has value.

The watch. The name. The proof, if he can keep enough of it dry.

He tells himself evidence is only useful if he survives to use it. He tells himself the Westly name has opened doors in the Tower all his life.

Maybe it can still open one down here.

Maybe, even on the Plains, someone will hear that name and decide he is worth pulling out.

### Node 2B

Sedes checks the camera again.

The casing is wet. The screen flickers. Half the footage may already be gone.

Still, he keeps it against his chest.

He is one person in a collapsing mine, with no guards, no council seat, and no guarantee anyone will believe him. But if the Tower can turn bodies into light, then someone has to carry the dark back up.

Even alone, he can still try to change a world that eats people.

### Node 2C

Sedes stays with the injured miner.

He keeps talking, even when his own breath starts to thin.

Look at me. Keep your hand above the water. Don't sleep. When I count, you move.

The miner curses him, then obeys.

Sedes braces one arm under his shoulder and waves others forward with the other. The camera knocks against his ribs. The watch cuts into his palm. For once, both feel less urgent than the man trying not to die beside him.

### Node3

The Plains rescue team reaches the upper shaft.

Ropes drop through the smoke. Floodlights cut across the acid water. For one impossible second, everyone looks up.

Then no one moves.

The miners look at Sedes: Tower-born, Westly-blooded, wearing what used to be the most expensive protective suit in the shaft. Now the armor is eaten through, the fabric smoking at the seams, his camera half-corroded against his chest.

The rescue team can pull people up.

But the miners decide who gets close enough to the ropes.

Sedes has one last chance to make them believe him.

### ENDING A — Westly's Shadow Survives.

Sedes speaks the family name like a key.

He promises Tower residency permits for the miners and their families. He promises council signatures, sealed documents, and a scandal large enough to make the Tower pay for silence.

Enough people believe the bargain.

The rescue team pulls him through.

The evidence becomes leverage.

The Westlys had never been powerless.

After the bargain, they become harder to touch.

More doors open. More councillors lower their voices when they speak. Old enemies begin sending invitations instead of threats.

The Tower calls it reform.

The council calls it compromise.

The newspapers call it the beginning of a gentler age.

Sedes tells himself this is protection.

His parents do not look protected.

His father reads the sealed agreements twice and says nothing. His mother sits very still beside the window, the charity banquet pearls cold against her throat.

At last, she asks him whether the dead miners were also part of the exchange.

Sedes has no answer.

On the Plains, doors close before he reaches them.

Some miners call him cold-blooded, a Tower-born man who could only understand bargains.

Others remember that he reached for them when the acid water rose.

Both can be true.

Years later, doors still open for him in the Tower.

On the Plains, they close before he can knock.

In the council hall, men who once looked through him now smile and make room at the table. They call him practical. They call him promising.

Sedes learns to hate how easily they welcome him.

He tells himself this is how survival works inside the Tower.

A cleaner truth would have died in the dark.

A dirtier one bought signatures, permits, and time.

Some miners live because of it.

His family lives better because of it.

Both can be true.

Sedes tells himself he can live with that.

Some nights, he almost believes it.

### ENDING B — The Lone Witness Survives.

Sedes throws his Tower ID into the acid water.

For a moment, the camera is in the foreman's hands.

Then it is pushed back into Sedes's chest.

The miners have long since stopped believing in the Tower, or in truth as something powerful enough to matter. But they see the fire in Sedes's eyes, and for the first time that night, some of them begin to hope despite themselves.

Not mercy from above.

Not justice handed down cleanly.

Only that perhaps this Tower-born boy might carry something back that could no longer be buried.

Sedes returns home with acid burns on his skin, blood under his nails, and a camera half-dead against his ribs.

He tells the Westlys everything.

For a long time, neither of his parents speaks.

They had spent years funding schools, hospitals, relief programs, charity banquets. They had believed, or needed to believe, that a gentler hand could soften the Tower's law.

Then his father asks, quietly:

“Are there copies?”

His mother closes her eyes.

When she opens them again, the polished grief is gone from her face.

From that moment on, the Westly family stands against the Tower.

The evidence is incomplete. The route upward is watched. The council will not let the truth surface without demanding a body for it.

But someone has to carry it.

Someone has to say that the light was made from people.

The truth gets out.

So does his name.

The evidence becomes a spark. The council cannot bury the scandal without turning its eyes on the Westlys.

His family stands beside him because they love him.

And because some truths must be carried, even when every road after them leads into gunfire.

Perhaps one day, a rifle will find them in some quiet corner of the Tower.

But none of them will regret the light they chose to stand in.

### ENDING C — Ghost of the Plains.

The miners choose to believe him.

Not because he is innocent of the Tower, or free from the Westly name, but because he once chose living bodies over proof, status, and escape.

Some people are born inside the Tower and still keep a human conscience.

A journalist's first duty is to see things as they are.

When the rescue count reaches the Tower, Sedes Westly is listed among the dead.

The evidence is damaged. The camera is gone. The official story ends there.

But the Plains give him another name.

A dead man has no seat in the council, no family shield, and no lawful path home.

He also has no leash.

Under a borrowed name, he begins to investigate what the Tower buried beneath the mines.

In the Tower, the Westlys receive a sealed report and a body that is not their son.

They are told Sedes died in the mine collapse.

His mother does not believe the report.

His father reads it once, then again, and folds it with hands that do not stop shaking.

They never learn where he went.

But grief needs somewhere to go.

In the months that follow, the Westlys pour money into mine rescue teams, emergency shelters, medical stations, and legal aid for families who are told their dead were only statistics.

The council calls it charity.

His mother calls it debt.

His father calls it the least a living house can do for a dead son.

### ENDING — No Trust.

Sedes reaches for the nearest hand.

No one takes it.

The miners have seen enough.

They saw him protect the Westly family's treasure. They saw him protect the evidence that could burn the Tower. They saw him look away when living people were suffering in front of him.

And now, at the edge of the ropes, he is hoping to be saved by the Plains people he ignored.

Of course no one reaches back.

In their eyes, Sedes is only another arrogant Tower-born man who remembers the lower world only when he needs it.

He tried to keep everything: family, evidence, and escape. In the end, he keeps nothing.

When the rescue team pulls away, the miners take what can still be sold: the camera, cracked but recognizable; the gold watch, heavy enough to buy food, medicine, passage, maybe a week of breathing room.

The photograph inside the watch slips free.

The young Westlys smile up from the yellowed paper for one last moment before the acid water takes them.

Sedes follows.

By morning, the Tower receives one more unit of clean light.

No name attached.

### AFTER ROPE LEVERAGE

The ropes drag Sedes into the open air.

For a moment, he cannot stand. Acid water runs from his sleeves. Someone cuts the ruined straps from his shoulders.

A miner catches him before he falls. The man's hand is rough, warm, and smeared dark with blood and coal dust.

Sedes feels the Westly watch still ticking against his ribs.

The miner looks at him for a long second.

"Plains leadership would be interested in someone like you," he says. "If you can live without hiding behind that name."

### AFTER ROPE SPARK

The ropes drag Sedes into the open air.

For a moment, he cannot stand. Acid water runs from his sleeves. Someone cuts the ruined straps from his shoulders.

A miner reaches out to steady him. The man's hand is rough, warm, and smeared dark with blood and coal dust.

Against Sedes's chest, the damaged camera gives one small mechanical click.

The miner looks from the camera to Sedes.

"The people beyond the plains would want to hear from someone like you." he says. "If you can decide whether you want to expose the Tower, or leave it behind."

## Side Route

In the Tower, everyone condemns the Plains black market in public.

In private, almost everyone uses it.

Parts that cannot be bought through official channels. Devices that should not be registered. Medicine, tools, forged permits, silence. The Tower survives partly because the Plains knows how to get things done.

After Sedes survives the mine disaster, a message arrives from an old contact.

Mara:

Your equipment is here. Pick it up before I sell it to someone with better manners.

So Sedes returns to Mara's workshop.

### Dinner

The workshop smells of oil, metal dust, fried food, and cheap alcohol.

Sedes expects a quick exchange: money for equipment, no questions asked.

Instead, he finds Mara at the dinner table with a girl who looks like she was placed there by mistake.

Elian.

Noelle's adopted daughter. Noelle's public miracle. Noelle's carefully trained answer to a public-relations crisis.

Officially, Elian is staying outside the Tower for her own safety, until the noise dies down. Unofficially, Noelle needed somewhere safe, unofficial, and far from cameras.

Mara was useful enough to trust.

Not respectable enough to respect.

Elian smiles too politely. Mara pours too much wine. Sedes realizes this is not a delivery pickup anymore.

It is the beginning of something already dangerous.

### Memory

Mara remembers the thirty-second day.

Elian had been living in the workshop for a month by then, long enough to learn how to bargain with wanted traders, drink cheap beer without flinching, and laugh with her whole face instead of the practiced smile Noelle had trained into her.

That night, they bought orange saplings for the yard.

"When they grow," Elian said, crouching beside the little trees, "leave some on the branches for me. I want to steal them when I get hungry."

"I'll leave half the tree," Mara said. "Take as many as you want."

Later, they ran.

No destination. No plan. Just a motorbike, dirty hair, canned beer, street food, boxing tickets, lost bets, cheap fireworks, bare feet on the beach, and a boat they absolutely could not afford.

For one impossible night, Elian was not a product, not a public image, not Noelle's perfect girl.

She was wind-burned, laughing, broke, alive.

Mara had thought: if I can give her this forever, maybe that is love.

### Confrontation

The camera turns to Mara.

Elian is still smiling, but the smile has started to tremble.

Sedes says nothing. For once, he knows this is not his scene to solve.

Mara looks at Elian and understands that love has finally become a question with teeth.

### ENDING A — The Walled Garden

Mara asks Noelle to make Elian stay.

She promises Elian freedom and happiness outside the cage, and Elian believes her because the promise sounds like the first life she has ever been allowed to choose.

They are too young then.

Young enough to trade a whole life for one bright moment of desire, and too dazzled to remember that every escape has a cost.

For a while, it works.

Elian is no longer a polished public image. She becomes messier, louder, more ordinary, more her own.

Noelle lets it happen.

Not because she is moved by love, but because love is useful. A Tower idol and a black-market trader: rebellious, authentic, cross-class, perfect for changing the public conversation.

For a season, the Tower talks about Noelle's adopted daughter bravely choosing love. Noelle becomes the open-minded guardian who let her go.

The crisis fades.

Then ordinary life arrives.

Different rhythms. Different habits. Different worlds. The glow that once blinded them begins to thin, and underneath it are bills, dishes, silence, and arguments that never quite end.

Not every love is meant to last a lifetime.

So they stay.

Not ruined. Not saved.

Only a little sand mixed into the love.

### ENDING B — The Moon Returns

Mara lets Elian go.

Not because it does not hurt.

A few days earlier, Noelle had come to the workshop with that calm capitalist face, speaking as if every wound was already priced.

Mara had almost overturned the workbench. For one second, she really thought about picking up a hammer and ending the conversation another way.

Noelle only looked at her and said Elian was never meant to stay here.

A Tower girl. A public image. A product, maybe. But not Mara's to keep.

Mara hated her for being right.

The moon does not come down just because someone loves it from the ground.

So Mara makes a trade.

For the next two years, everything she sells Noelle will be charged at cost. No profit. No tricks. No black-market premium.

In return, Noelle agrees to three conditions.

Elian chooses her own debut image.

No more fake-smile training.

And whatever love the public gives her, she receives it as herself.

Mara asks for one more thing: let her eat until she is full.

Noelle says no.

Mara swears at her, takes the three conditions, and promises that if Noelle breaks them, the next negotiation will involve a hammer.

Elian goes back to the Tower.

Back to the stage. Back to the cameras. Back to Noelle.

But not exactly back to the cage.

Mara never holds what she loves.

She only leaves the door less locked than before.

### ENDING C — One Eternal Moment

Mara takes Elian and runs.

No contract. No permission. No clever bargain dressed up as sacrifice.

For one night, love becomes motion.

By dawn, Noelle's people find them on the Plains.

The story ends before it can become ordinary.

Both bodies are left under a sky Elian was never supposed to touch.

### EpilogueA

Sedes returns to pick up his equipment.

Mara and Elian are arguing about something small: a burnt pan, a missing receipt, a door left unlocked.

He does not ask what happened.

On the table, two oranges sit in a chipped bowl.

Neither of them has been eaten.

### EpilogueB

Sedes returns to the workshop.

A ribbon hangs from the coat rack. Half the oranges are still on the tree.

On the wall, a poster: Elian's face, studio-lit and perfect.

Mara is underneath it, working.

She does not look up.

### EpilogueC

Sedes returns to the workshop.

It is abandoned.

The tools are gone. The orange tree is gone. Only a dark square on the wall shows where Elian's poster used to be.

He will need a new black-market contact.
